## THE INSIGHT

The Shizuoka Method evaluates if a business idea can make money.
We evaluate if a social impact idea can **change lives**.

That's not the same thing. A business just needs customers.
A social impact idea needs:
- A real problem (not a fad)
- Cultural fit (what works in Japan fails in DRC)
- Community trust (you can't buy this)
- Zero-budget viability (most social entrepreneurs have nothing)
- A path from idea → proof → mentorship → funding

**No tool in the world does this.** That's our innovation.

## THE USER JOURNEY

Not "input → score → done." A complete pathway:

### STAGE 1: THE IDEA
**User has:** A dream. "I want to help elderly people in my village."
**We give:** Structure. "What's the problem? What's your goal? Where? What do you have?"

### STAGE 2: THE EVALUATION
**User gets:** Honest assessment. "Here's what's working. Here's what's in your way. Here's your score."
**We give:** Grounded feedback. Not "try harder" but "partner with the local temple. They already have trust."

### STAGE 3: THE PROOF
**User gets:** A 2-week plan. "Do this on Day 1. This on Day 3. Serve 10 people. If 7 say yes — you have proof."
**We give:** Specific, actionable steps. Not theory. "Find 3 isolated seniors. Ask: when was the last time someone checked on you?"

### STAGE 4: THE MENTOR (Phase 2)
**User gets:** Matched to a mentor. Someone who did something similar in a similar context.
**We give:** Connection to the 57 influential figures in our database. "Kumi Imamura built KATARIBA. Here's what she learned."

### STAGE 5: THE FUND (Phase 2)
**User gets:** Matched to funding. "Based on your score, country, and idea type — here are 3 funding sources."
**We give:** Specific pathways. "NLnet Foundation: EUR 5K-50K. Apply here. Here's what they look for."

## THE REPORT — REDESIGNED

Not a scorecard. A conversation with a mentor who's been there.

### Section 1: THE HONEST ANSWER
"What you told us: [their pitch]. Here's what we think: [verdict]."

Tone by score:
- **8+**: "This is ready. Stop planning. Here's Day 1."
- **6-7.9**: "You're close. One thing is holding you back. Fix it."
- **4-5.9**: "The idea is good. The approach needs to change."
- **Below 4**: "This is hard. But here's someone who did harder."

### Section 2: YOUR SDG IMPACT
"Your idea aligns with these UN Sustainable Development Goals:"

Map every idea to 1-3 SDGs with specific targets:
- Health idea → SDG 3 (Good Health) + Target 3.8 (Universal health coverage)
- Education idea → SDG 4 (Quality Education) + Target 4.6 (Literacy)
- Water idea → SDG 6 (Clean Water) + Target 6.1 (Safe drinking water)
- Work idea → SDG 8 (Decent Work) + Target 8.5 (Full employment)
- Women idea → SDG 5 (Gender Equality) + Target 5.5 (Women's leadership)
- Food idea → SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) + Target 2.1 (End hunger)

Show: "If you serve 100 people, here's your SDG impact score."

### Section 3: YOUR MARKET
- Country context (Hofstede dimensions, but presented as practical advice)
- Economic tier (what technology works)
- Trust layer (who people already trust)
- What already works here (case studies from this zone)

### Section 4: YOUR OBSTACLES
- Specific barriers with specific workarounds
- Not "Power Distance is 70" but "People won't challenge authority. Partner with the temple/mosque/church."
- Each barrier gets: The Problem → Why It Matters → How to Fix It

### Section 5: YOUR BLUEPRINT
- Someone who did something similar (case study)
- What they did right
- What they did wrong
- What you should copy
- What you should avoid

### Section 6: YOUR 2-WEEK SPRINT
- Day-by-day plan
- Idea-type-specific
- $0 budget
- Success criteria

### Section 7: YOUR NEXT STEP
- "Do this today."
- "If it works, apply here for funding."
- "If it doesn't, try this instead."

## THE SDG INTEGRATION

### Why SDGs Matter
1. **Credibility**: Funders (NLnet, Mozilla, UN agencies) think in SDGs
2. **Measurement**: "I helped 50 people" is vague. "I advanced SDG 3.8 in rural Bangladesh" is specific.
3. **Network**: SDG-aligned organizations can find and support each other
4. **Impact**: Forces the user to think about MEASURABLE outcomes

### How It Works
Every idea gets mapped to SDGs based on idea type:

| Idea Type | Primary SDG | Secondary SDG | Key Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health | SDG 3: Good Health | SDG 1: No Poverty | 3.8: Universal coverage |
| Education | SDG 4: Quality Education | SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities | 4.6: Literacy |
| Food | SDG 2: Zero Hunger | SDG 3: Good Health | 2.1: End hunger |
| Water | SDG 6: Clean Water | SDG 3: Good Health | 6.1: Safe drinking water |
| Safety | SDG 16: Peace & Justice | SDG 5: Gender Equality | 16.1: Reduce violence |
| Work | SDG 8: Decent Work | SDG 1: No Poverty | 8.5: Full employment |
| Financial | SDG 1: No Poverty | SDG 8: Decent Work | 1.4: Equal rights to resources |
| Women | SDG 5: Gender Equality | SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities | 5.5: Women's leadership |
| Elderly | SDG 3: Good Health | SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities | 3.4: Reduce mortality |
| Mental Health | SDG 3: Good Health | SDG 16: Peace & Justice | 3.4: Mental health |
| Disaster | SDG 13: Climate Action | SDG 11: Sustainable Cities | 13.1: Disaster resilience |
| Community | SDG 11: Sustainable Cities | SDG 16: Peace & Justice | 11.4: Cultural heritage |

### Impact Scoring
```
SDG Impact Score = (People Served × SDG Weight × Cultural Fit) / Budget

Where:
- People Served = estimated reach
- SDG Weight = how critical this SDG target is (1-10)
- Cultural Fit = Hofstede compatibility (0-1)
- Budget = total investment needed
```

A $0 idea that serves 50 people with high cultural fit scores HIGHER than a $10,000 idea that serves 100 people with low cultural fit. Because the $0 idea is REPRODUCIBLE.

## THE INPUT — REDESIGNED

Not a form. A conversation.

### Field 1: THE PROBLEM
"What's broken? What suffering exists?"
- Required
- 30+ characters
- Example: "Elderly people in rural Japan live alone. No one checks on them. Depression is rising."

### Field 2: THE GOAL
"What does success look like? Be specific."
- Required
- 30+ characters
- Example: "Connect 50 isolated seniors with daily WhatsApp check-ins from volunteers."

### Field 3: THE LOCATION
"Where is this happening?"
- Required
- Free text (country, city, region)
- Example: "Rural Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan"

### Field 4: THE RESOURCES
"What do you have? Budget, team, skills, connections."
- Optional
- Free text
- Example: "$0. Just me and my phone. I know 3 neighbors who might help."

### Field 5: THE CONSTRAINTS
"What's stopping you? What are you afraid of?"
- Optional
- Free text
- Example: "Seniors don't use smartphones. Cultural shame around asking for help."

### Example Chips (auto-fill all fields)
- "Indian street food in London"
- "Elder care WhatsApp in Japan"
- "Sanitary pads in Bangladesh"
- "Coding classes in Nairobi"
- "Clean water for schools in Kenya"
- "Mental health support for students in Korea"

## THE DESIGN — INNOVATED

### Not a Landing Page. A Tool.

The Shizuoka Method has a landing page that generates ideas.
We have a TOOL that evaluates YOUR idea.

The difference:
- Shizuoka: "Here are 3 ideas for your problem"
- Us: "Here's what we think about YOUR idea"

### Visual Language
- **Dark** — serious, not playful. Social impact is serious.
- **Glowing** — hopeful, not corporate. People have dreams.
- **Structured** — clear sections, not overwhelming. Like a report from a trusted advisor.
- **Personal** — uses their words, their context, their constraints.

### The Report Reads Like a Letter

Not:
```
LAYER 3: CULTURAL MATRIX
Power Distance: 70 (MEDIUM barrier)
Individualism: 20 (LOW barrier)
```

But:
```
Here's what you're up against in [country]:

People here don't challenge authority. That means you can't go around
the local leaders — you have to go THROUGH them. Partner with the
temple, the mosque, the school. They already have the trust you need.

The good news: community bonds are strong. If you can get one person
to vouch for you, the network effect kicks in.
```

## THE BUSINESS MODEL

### Free Forever for Users
- No paywall
- No sign-up
- No data collection
- Open source

### Revenue (Phase 3)
- **Funding matching**: Take 5% of successfully matched grants
- **Mentorship platform**: Premium mentor access ($50/session)
- **Institutional API**: Universities, NGOs, governments pay for bulk access
- **SDG reporting**: Organizations pay for impact reports aligned with UN frameworks

### Impact Metrics (what we report to funders)
- Ideas evaluated per month
- Ideas that moved to proof-of-work stage
- Ideas that received funding
- People served by funded ideas
- SDG targets advanced
