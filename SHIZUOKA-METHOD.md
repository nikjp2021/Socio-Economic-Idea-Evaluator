# The Shizuoka Method

## Origin

The Shizuoka Method is a structured framework for evaluating socio-economic ideas. It was developed by Nikhil Tiwari during his PhD research at Shizuoka University's Nagayoshi Lab, Japan. The method combines cultural dimensions theory, economic tier classification, and community viability testing into a single evaluation pipeline.

The method was created to fill a gap: business ideas have hundreds of evaluation tools (business model canvas, lean canvas, pitch decks). Social impact ideas had none. The Shizuoka Method evaluates whether an idea can change lives — not just make money.

---

## Philosophy

**秩序と創造 — Order and Creation**

The user brings the Order: a structured description of their idea (the problem, the goal, the constraints). The engine brings the Creation: a multi-layered evaluation grounded in cultural data, economic reality, and real-world evidence.

Neither works alone. Order without Creation is a dream. Creation without Order is noise.

## The 5 Principles

### 1. Structured Input, Not Free Text

The user describes their idea through 5 fields:
- **Problem** — What's broken in their community
- **Goal** — What they want to achieve
- **Country** — Where they're operating
- **Budget** — What they have
- **Constraints** — Team size, time, tools

This is not a form to fill out. It's a conversation starter. The engine parses these fields to detect the country, idea type, and economic tier automatically.

### 2. FAD Filtering

Not every problem is persistent. Some are trends. Some are media noise. The Shizuoka Method distinguishes between:

- **Structural problems** — poverty, lack of healthcare, gender inequality. These have existed for decades. Low fad risk.
- **Emerging problems** — mental health awareness, digital literacy. These are real but newer. Medium fad risk.
- **Trend-driven problems** — tied to a specific technology or cultural moment. High fad risk.

The FAD filter ensures the evaluator doesn't confuse "popular right now" with "important forever."

### 3. Personalized Verdicts by Score Band

Every evaluation produces a score from 1 to 10. The score maps to one of four verdicts:

| Score | Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 8.0+ | **GO** | Ready to test. Start Day 1. |
| 6.0–7.9 | **GO WITH EDUCATION** | Close, but something is holding you back. Fix it, then test. |
| 4.0–5.9 | **PIVOT** | The idea is good. The approach needs to change. |
| Below 4.0 | **SHELVE** | This is hard. Revisit with a different angle or more resources. |

Each verdict comes with a personalized explanation using the user's own words, specific barriers, and a concrete next step. No generic advice.

### 4. Case Study Grounding

Every evaluation is matched to real-world evidence. The system searches 165 case studies across 10 global zones and 12 idea categories. The matching uses a 3-mode system:

- **Mode 1 — Exact Match:** A real organization did something similar in a similar context. Their story, numbers, and lessons are presented directly.
- **Mode 2 — Hypothetical (Grounded):** No exact match exists. The system finds the 3 closest real organizations, extracts their proven principles, and builds a hypothetical applying those principles to the user's context. Explicitly marked as hypothetical.
- **Mode 3 — Novel:** No precedent exists. The user is creating something new. This is stated honestly, not as a failure.

Every case study includes: what worked, what didn't, and one key lesson.

### 5. Bootstrapper Voice

The evaluator speaks like a co-founder, not a professor. The tone is direct, specific, and actionable. It uses the user's own words. It doesn't hedge with "it depends" or "further research is needed."

The bootstrapper principle: every recommendation must answer the question "Can a 2-person team with no budget actually do this?"

## The V3 Framework

The Shizuoka Method is implemented through the V3 Framework — three dimensions that every social impact idea must pass:

### Viability — Can This Work?

Tested by:
- **Three Community Tests** — Would a WhatsApp group already solve this? Can you serve 10 people? Does it survive on zero tech?
- **Bootstrapper Score** — How easy, feasible, and effortful is this to start?

### Velocity — How Fast Can This Grow?

Tested by:
- **Cultural Fit** — 6 Hofstede dimensions scored against the target country. Identifies specific barriers and whether they're trainable.
- **Education Lever** — Which barriers can be overcome with training? What's the ROI of education?

### Value — What's the Impact?

Tested by:
- **SDG Mapping** — Every idea mapped to 1-3 UN Sustainable Development Goals with specific targets.
- **Impact Score** — A formula combining SDG weight, cultural fit, and estimated reach.
- **FAD Risk** — Is this problem persistent or trending?

## The 7-Layer Pipeline

The Shizuoka Method is implemented as a 7-layer evaluation pipeline:

```
Layer 1: PARSE & STRUCTURE
  Input → Country, Idea Type, Economic Tier

Layer 2: THREE COMMUNITY TESTS
  Facebook Group Test, 10-for-10 Test, WhatsApp-Only Test

Layer 3: CULTURAL MATRIX
  6 Hofstede Dimensions → Barriers → Compatibility Score

Layer 4: EDUCATION LEVER
  Barriers → Trainable vs. Structural → Education ROI

Layer 5: BOOTSTRAPPER SCORE
  Easy / Feasible / Efforts → Nikhil's Take

Layer 6: CASE STUDY MATCHING
  165 cases → 3-mode matching → Narrative + Expert Insight

Layer 7: VERDICT & PROOF-OF-WORK
  Score → Verdict → Pitch → 2-Week Plan → Funding Pathway
```

Each layer feeds into the next. The final score is a weighted combination:

```
Total = (Community × 0.30) + (Cultural × 0.15) + (Education × 0.15)
      + (Bootstrapper × 0.20) + (Impact × 0.20)
```

## Data Foundation

The Shizuoka Method is grounded in:

- **136 countries** with Hofstede cultural dimension scores (PDI, IDV, MAS, UAI, LTO, IVR)
- **10 countries** with rich cultural profiles (trust layers, community types, what works, what fails)
- **10 global zones** with aggregated cultural and economic data
- **165 case studies** of real social impact organizations across 55+ countries
- **57 influential figures** in social impact
- **17 UN Sustainable Development Goals** with specific targets

## How to Reference

When describing the Shizuoka Method in documentation, UI, or communications:

**Do say:**
- "The Shizuoka Method — a structured framework for evaluating social impact ideas"
- "Developed through PhD research at Shizuoka University, Japan"
- "Combines cultural dimensions, economic tiers, and community viability testing"

**Do not say:**
- Any comparison to trademarked brands, institutions, or shows
- "Harvard-level" or "academic-grade" (implies institutional affiliation)
- "Like [Brand] for social impact" (implies endorsement)

Describe what the method DOES, not what it's LIKE.
